How Maps Invented the Modern World

Mira Vale and Theo Arlen examine how surveying instruments, cadastral records, colonial conferences, and administrative maps turned uncertain frontiers into lines that governments could tax, police, and defend. Maps did more than represent territory: they helped manufacture the modern idea that sovereignty should fit inside exact, continuous borders. The episode moves from triangulation and boundary monuments to the partition of India, colonial boundary-making, and today's satellite coordinate systems. Along the way, it asks what gets hidden when a complicated landscape is reduced to a line, and why borders can feel natural long after the political decisions that created them have been forgotten.
