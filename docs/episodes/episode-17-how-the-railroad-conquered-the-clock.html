How the Railroad Conquered the Clock

Before standard time, noon arrived whenever the sun crossed each town's sky. Railroads, telegraphs, factories, and national markets made that local freedom increasingly unworkable. This episode follows the railroad conventions that synchronized North American clocks, the 1884 International Meridian Conference, the Standard Time Act, and the persistent fight over daylight saving time.
