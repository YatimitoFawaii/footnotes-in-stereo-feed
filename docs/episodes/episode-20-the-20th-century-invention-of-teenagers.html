The 20th Century Invention of Teenagers

Before "teenager" became a marketing category, it had to be built by schools, labor law, mass media, and consumer culture. This episode follows the social machinery that separated adolescence from childhood and adulthood: child labor reform, compulsory education, the expansion of high school, youth magazines, pop culture, and the postwar marketplace that learned to sell directly to young people. The story starts with reformers fighting to pull children out of dangerous industrial work, then moves through the high school movement that made adolescence a shared institution rather than a short private transition. From there, the episode tracks how music, magazines, movies, and advertisers turned young people into a visible public audience with its own tastes, anxieties, and purchasing power.
